## Description (paste as-is)
```
At 2:13 in the morning, the bell rings at pump four. There's no car. Just a man hugging the nozzle, staring at the glass… and all he can see is himself.

You're Sam, the night clerk at Sunny's, mile 88 on Route 9, the last gas station before the desert. The outbreak started on a Tuesday. A woman left her daughter at your door and said three things: "Her name is Junie. Keep the lights on. I'll find you."

Thirty nights later, the dead are still walking past. As long as the canopy stays bright, they can't see inside. But the power just died. And the only gasoline for forty miles is twenty thousand gallons… under your feet.

This is a fictional survival story. The real-world details in it are true:
- Gas stations have an emergency shutoff that cuts power to every pump at once (NFPA 30A).
- At night, a window acts like a mirror for whoever is on the brighter side, the same physics as a one-way mirror.
- Plan for one gallon of water per person per day (FEMA / Ready.gov).
- Always fill a gas can on the ground, never in a truck bed or car; static electricity can ignite fumes (Petroleum Equipment Institute).
- Gas-station fuel is stored in underground tanks, and the pumps need electricity to bring it up (US EPA).
- Walk-in coolers are usually built with an inside safety release (design varies; check yours).

Would you have flipped the lights on? Tell me in the comments.

New episode every Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

```
