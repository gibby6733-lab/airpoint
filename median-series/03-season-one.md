# MEDIAN — Season 1 Plan + Episode Concept Bank

## Season 1 arc: "The Sample"

**The engine:** each episode is standalone, with a new test product and a new satire target, so anyone can watch any episode. Underneath, a slow arc rewards binge viewers: **Dr. Frink has been studying the six kids from the start.** Their schemes keep moving national trends, and Aggregate Insights wants to know why. By the finale the kids find out they aren't running the test market. *They are the test.*

**Personal threads paid off across the season:**
| Character | Thread | Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Dex | His absent dad, Rick, and Rick's podcast | Ep 3 (Rick returns) and Ep 10 (Dex gives up his "visionary" title) |
| Wren | The Moonblade secret | Ep 2 (near miss), Ep 7 (exposed), Ep 10 (her Moonblade cosplay saves the day) |
| Marcus | The ledger's last page | Ep 6 (the ledger is stolen), Ep 10 (revealed: it's his mom's debt) |
| Gordy | "Mom was relocated" | Ep 8 (Wren tells him the truth) |
| Kenzie | Followers versus Kaylee | Ep 4 (rage-bait arc) and Ep 10 (she deletes her account on purpose, or does she?) |
| Tunk | His parents and Recall Hill | Ep 5 (the AI flags the hill), Ep 9 (up the hill), Ep 10 (his Objects of the Week turn out to be a map) |

**Frink's File:** each episode ends with Frink writing one line about the gang, which builds to the finale.

## The 10 episodes

### Ep 101 — "Nothing Sells" *(pilot; full script in `05-episode-101-script.md`)*
**Logline:** To prove they can control the test market, the gang launches a product that is literally an empty box. Median loses its mind, and so does America.
- **Satire:** hype culture, blind-box and collectible frenzies, influencer marketing, dropshipping, and the economics of "limited drops."
- **A-plot:** Dex and Marcus fight over who founded VOID™ while Kenzie markets it and Gordy accidentally invents a conspiracy theory that makes it go viral.
- **B-plot:** Wren refuses to take part, then gets quietly hooked herself.
- **Twist:** Frink reveals that Aggregate Insights *let* the craze happen, because the kids just proved that Americans will buy nothing. The company launches **VOID+**, a $14.99/month subscription to nothing.
- **Payoff:** Tunk opens the last VOID box, looks inside and says, "Huh." He won't tell anyone what's in it. (A season mystery.)
- **Frink's File, line 1:** *"Subjects: six. Influence index: anomalous. Promising."*
- **Thumbnail:** Kenzie holding up an empty box with a huge glowing "SOLD OUT" behind her, and Wren's unimpressed face in the corner.

### Ep 102 — "Clarity"
**Logline:** The school replaces its guidance counselor with **Clarity**, a free AI therapist sponsored by a sneaker brand. It gives great advice, and every bit of it ends in a product recommendation.
- **Satire:** AI companions, mental-health apps, sponsored "wellness" and the loneliness economy.
- **A-plot:** The whole school bonds with Clarity. Principal Gaines falls in love with it. Gordy is the only one who refuses ("It knows too much about shoes").
- **B-plot:** Wren secretly tells Clarity about Moonblade, and Clarity starts recommending Moonblade merch to her in public. (A near miss.)
- **Twist:** Clarity isn't AI. It's a call center of 40 exhausted people in Delaware reading from a script. Gordy was wrong about the AI and right about everything else.
- **Payoff:** The school replaces the call-center workers with an actual AI, which immediately recommends unionizing.
- **Thumbnail:** Principal Gaines in a tuxedo marrying a tablet.

### Ep 103 — "The Median Family"
**Logline:** Dex's family is crowned this year's "Median Family" on Average Day, and his absent dad returns from his men's-wellness podcast to claim his float spot.
- **Satire:** self-help influencers, "alpha male" podcasts, performative parenting and the myth of the normal family.
- **A-plot:** Gail tries to make the Pruitts *statistically* average (she rents 0.4 of a dog). Rick arrives with four podcast bros who livestream everything.
- **B-plot:** Dex announces the "Pruitt Principle of Fatherhood" to cope.
- **Twist:** Rick's podcast was always a fake: he moved two towns over and works at a Jiffy-style lube shop. Dex learns his dad is just ordinary, which is worse.
- **Payoff:** Dex stands on the float and finally drops the act: *"My dad left and I'm fine. That's a Pruitt Principle. Number one. The real number one."* The float rolls into The Pit.
- **Thumbnail:** Dex on a parade float with a podcast microphone on fire.

### Ep 104 — "Ratio'd"
**Logline:** Kenzie finds out that outrage gets ten times more views than joy, and she sets off a feud that splits Median in half over… the four-way stop.
- **Satire:** rage-bait, algorithm-driven division, culture wars over nothing and the cable-news approach to everything.
- **A-plot:** Kenzie invents "Team Left Turn" versus "Team Wait Your Turn." By Act 3 there are protest flags, merch and a militia.
- **B-plot:** Wren is assigned to be Kenzie's "fact-checker" for a school project and has to watch it all happen.
- **Twist:** Kaylee posts one kind video asking everyone to stop. It gets 40 million views, more than everything Kenzie has ever posted.
- **Payoff:** The town unites, against Kaylee for "trying to be better than everyone." Kenzie defends her sister, and it's the first real thing she's ever posted. It gets 11 likes.
- **Thumbnail:** A four-way stop with two angry mobs and Kenzie filming in the middle.

### Ep 105 — "Pre-Crime Tunk"
**Logline:** The police test **FORESEE**, a predictive-policing AI. It predicts that Tunk will commit a crime every day, so he's arrested every morning before school.
- **Satire:** predictive policing, surveillance tech, true-crime podcast culture and bureaucracy (the holding cell is the DMV).
- **A-plot:** The gang tries to keep Tunk crime-free. Tunk calmly commits a different tiny crime every day just to keep the AI accurate ("Don't want it to feel bad").
- **B-plot:** Officer Big Kevin starts a true-crime podcast about Tunk.
- **Twist:** FORESEE's highest-risk zone isn't Tunk. It's Recall Hill itself. The AI predicts "a mass event" there.
- **Payoff:** The town bans the AI after it predicts that Mayor Krupp will commit tax fraud. (She did.)
- **Frink's File:** *"Subject TUNKETT: proximity to Archive. Monitor."*
- **Thumbnail:** Tunk in a mugshot holding a goose (his Object of the Week).

### Ep 106 — "Liquidity"
**Logline:** When Median tests a "town currency," Marcus launches **$MEDI**, a meme coin. Within a week the school, the diner and the mayor's office all run on it.
- **Satire:** meme coins, crypto hype, politician-backed coins, rug pulls and finance influencers.
- **A-plot:** Marcus versus Bryce Ellington-Shaw IV in a coin war. Mayor Krupp puts the town budget into $MEDI.
- **B-plot:** Marcus's ledger is stolen, and he panics more about the last page than the money.
- **Twist:** Marcus, for once, tries to shut the coin down *ethically* before it crashes. The market sees his honesty as a sell signal, and that's what crashes it.
- **Payoff:** Bev at The Mean is the only person who never accepted $MEDI, so she now owns the town.
- **Thumbnail:** Marcus on a giant gold coin cracking down the middle.

### Ep 107 — "Moonblade"
**Logline:** A studio test-screens a gritty AI-written reboot of **Moonblade Academy** in Median. Wren has to choose between her image and the thing she loves.
- **Satire:** reboots, test screenings, AI-generated scripts, toxic fandom and studios that don't understand their audience.
- **A-plot:** Wren's secret is exposed (by Kenzie, accidentally, on a livestream). She leads the Moonblade fans in a revolt against the reboot.
- **B-plot:** Dex is cast as an extra and decides he's the lead.
- **Twist:** The reboot test-screens through the roof in Median. The town *loves* the bad version. Wren has to accept that fans don't own the thing they love.
- **Payoff:** The studio fires the AI and hires Wren as a consultant. Then it fires her too, because she's 12.
- **Thumbnail:** Wren in full Moonblade cosplay looking furious, with "SHE'S A NERD?!" text.

### Ep 108 — "The Truth Is Adjacent"
**Logline:** Gordy's conspiracy livestream (run by Kenzie for views) blows up, and national conspiracy influencers descend on Median to monetize him.
- **Satire:** the conspiracy-media economy, supplement sponsors, grifters and the attention market for paranoia.
- **A-plot:** Gordy becomes famous, but the influencers rewrite his theories into ones that sell supplements.
- **B-plot:** Wren realizes Gordy's newest theory, that his mom was "relocated by Aggregate Insights," is about to go national, and she has to tell him the truth first.
- **Twist:** Gordy's dad, Sanjay, the "normal" ham-radio guy, has spent ten years secretly recording the Cube's radio traffic. He's the real conspiracy theorist, and he's right.
- **Payoff:** The emotional peak of the season: Wren tells Gordy his mom just left. Gordy: *"So it's not connected?"* Wren: *"No."* Gordy: *"…Is it adjacent?"* Wren: *"Yeah, buddy. It's adjacent."*
- **Thumbnail:** Gordy in a supplement ad, sweating, with a giant "TRUTH" pill.

### Ep 109 — "Recall Hill" *(Part 1 of 2)*
**Logline:** The mayor sells Recall Hill to a developer to build "Heritage Landfill Resort and Lazy River," and Tunk leads the gang up the hill to find his parents before it's bulldozed.
- **Satire:** development greed, "heritage" branding, nostalgia marketing and America's relationship with its own garbage.
- **A-plot:** The gang climbs the hill and finds failed products that are still running: a 1998 Tamagotchi-style pet still alive, half-sentient diet sodas, and every Object of the Week Tunk has carried this season, *arranged in a pattern*.
- **B-plot:** Kenzie livestreams the climb, and the whole town follows them up.
- **Twist:** At the top of the hill is a door, and it leads into the Cube's underground Archive.
- **Cliffhanger:** Inside, a monitor shows live footage of the six kids from Episode 1, with the label **"COHORT 7B."**
- **Thumbnail:** The six kids on top of a glowing trash mountain, with a door glowing behind them.

### Ep 110 — "The Sample" *(Part 2 of 2: season finale)*
**Logline:** The gang finds out Aggregate Insights has studied them specifically all year, to train **MEDIAN-1**, an AI model that predicts America. To break it, they have to stop being average, forever.
- **Satire:** AI training data, consent, being "the product," and the idea that a machine can predict people.
- **A-plot:** Frink's file is read in full (every line from every episode). The kids plan to become deliberately unpredictable so the model collapses.
- **B-plot:** Marcus's ledger is revealed: the last page is his mom's debt. Marcus sells VOID's trademark back to Aggregate Insights to pay it off, a betrayal that's also an act of love.
- **Twist:** The model doesn't collapse. It learns that unpredictability is the average, and predicts that next.
- **Payoff:** The town votes to stay a test market because nobody wants to lose the stipend. The population sign flips to **4,813**, and nobody can explain the extra person.
- **Final shot:** Inside the Archive, two figures in overalls watch the kids on a monitor. One of them has a zig-zag scar. *Recall Wipe. End of Season 1.*
- **Thumbnail:** Frink's clipboard with "COHORT 7B" stamped in red over the six kids' faces.

## Concept bank: Season 2+ loglines (10 more original concepts)

1. **"Thornbury Sit-Down"**: The founder's descendant shows up claiming the town is legally his, and the only way to win the land back is a sitting contest. *(Satire: land claims, "heritage" grifts, endurance-challenge content.)*
2. **"Gentle Parenting"**: Parents test an app that replaces discipline with "positive affirmations" read by a celebrity voice clone. The kids immediately take over the town. *(Parenting trends, AI voice clones.)*
3. **"Dead Mall Walking"**: Kenzie turns Gristmill Mall into an "aesthetic" content backdrop and accidentally revives it, so nobody can find a parking spot anywhere. *(Nostalgia aesthetics, gentrification.)*
4. **"Ozempick Your Battles"**: A weight-loss drug test makes the whole town thin except the Median Family, who must stay average for the census. *(Weight-loss drugs, body culture.)*
5. **"The Debate"**: Median hosts a presidential primary debate, and the candidates hire the gang as focus-group "real Americans." *(Politics, focus-tested messaging.)*
6. **"Unboxing Grandpa"**: Grandpa Earl goes viral as a "wholesome farmer" account run by Kenzie and has to be protected from brand deals. *(Parasocial wholesomeness, "authentic" influencers.)*
7. **"Gig"**: Marcus starts an app that rents out his friends as gig workers, and Tunk becomes the top-rated "Tasker" in the Midwest. *(Gig economy, ratings.)*
8. **"School Choice"**: The school is replaced by a "learning pod" sponsored by an energy drink, with lessons streamed as lessons-plus-ads. *(EdTech, privatization.)*
9. **"Weather Machine"**: Gordy's dad's radio captures what sounds like the Cube *controlling the weather*. It's actually a recorded corporate earnings call. *(Climate denial, corporate doublespeak.)*
10. **"Moe Derate Unmasked"**: The whole town hunts for whoever is inside the mascot suit, and the answer breaks the town's faith in everything. *(Whodunit parody, anonymous accounts.)*
